## `-d` — detach

Run the containers in the background and hand your prompt back immediately.

Without it (what [README:101](README.md#L101) uses), `docker compose up` stays in the foreground and streams every container's logs to your terminal — that's the behavior described at [README:161](README.md#L161). Ctrl+C then stops the whole stack.

With `-d`, you get your prompt back and read logs on demand:

```powershell
docker compose logs -f backend
```

Neither is more correct. Foreground is nice when you want to watch the backend reload as you edit; detached is nice when you want the terminal for other work. The one real difference: with `-d`, closing that terminal leaves the stack running; in the foreground, closing it takes the stack down with it.
